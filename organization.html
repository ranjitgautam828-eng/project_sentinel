Fraser Valley Sentinel · $1 billion pandemic preparedness initiative

05 · Organizational Context

Three spheres of system management, four frames of organization

Three spheres applied to Fraser Valley Sentinel

Schwalbe (2015) describes systems management as balancing the business, organizational, and technology spheres, so that a project is judged against the whole system it sits in and not only its technical output. The three cards below apply that model.

Business sphere

The proposed $1B envelope can be compared with the estimated $242B Canadian COVID-19 losses for context (Cotton, 2026); this is not a return forecast. The wastewater findings are modeled evidence (Government of Canada, 2026), not a local benefits estimate. Scope and procurement decisions remain subject to feasibility and approval.

Organization sphere

Fraser Health, BCCDC, UFV, the First Nations Health Authority, affected First Nations, and municipal governments may hold partial authority. No single organization can mandate the network. The project office is therefore a proposed convening structure, not a command structure; partner roles and any MOUs remain to be negotiated.

Technology sphere

Wastewater sequencing, AI anomaly detection, mRNA platform manufacturing, Far-UVC fixtures, and CRISPR-based at-home tests. The technology sphere is where the project’s leverage is highest. However, it is also where validation and interoperability risks concentrate.

Four frames applied to UFV and Fraser Health

Bolman and Deal (2017) argue that organizations are best understood through four lenses: structural, human resource, political, and symbolic. Using all four helps explain why a technically sound plan can still fail inside a university–health authority partnership.

Structural frame

The proposed model assigns UFV a research and platform role, Fraser Health a clinical-response role, and BCCDC a standards role. These roles require partner agreement. A provisional RACI matrix (below) can support those discussions; the structural risk is role drift at handoffs.

Human resource frame

The proposed team would need bioinformaticians, epidemiologists, lab technicians, project managers, community liaisons, and procurement specialists. UFV co-op and research assistant programs are potential workforce channels, subject to institutional agreement and capacity.

Political frame

Resources are scarce, goals are contested, and authority is fragmented. The airport and border jurisdictions are especially sensitive. The political frame says: do not assume alignment; negotiate it explicitly, and give every powerful stakeholder a visible stake in success.

Symbolic frame

The project’s meaning matters. “Fraser Valley Sentinel” is a symbol of regional self-reliance and regional collaboration. Any public milestone should be reported only after it is achieved and approved for release.
