Fraser Valley Sentinel · $1 billion pandemic preparedness initiative

08 · Risk, SWOT, Procurement & Quality

What can go wrong, what we buy, and how we test it

SWOT analysis

Strengths

  • Potential UFV research and talent contribution, subject to agreement.
  • Existing BCCDC wastewater surveillance provides regional context; access or integration is unconfirmed (BCCDC, 2026).
  • Strong regional agriculture/logistics base.
  • Potential regional partners, none yet confirmed.

Weaknesses

  • Fragmented jurisdictional authority.
  • No existing local mRNA manufacturing workforce.
  • Public fatigue with surveillance and health measures.
  • Dependence on external vendors for core hardware.

Opportunities

  • Federal/provincial pandemic preparedness funding alignment.
  • Modular manufacturing cost declines (Xtalks, 2022).
  • Far-UVC becoming cost-viable at scale (Far UV Technologies, 2026).
  • UFV can become a national model for regional preparedness.

Threats

  • Political funding instability across election cycles.
  • Vaccine nationalism and supply chain seizure.
  • Cost overruns consistent with project baselines (Flyvbjerg et al., 2002).
  • A pathogen outside the prototype family coverage.

Procurement management: what we buy, and what UFV needs.

If approved, the project would coordinate multiple suppliers rather than manufacture every component itself. Potential procurements include the following.

  • Sequencing sensors, reagents, and maintenance: multi-year framework agreements with uptime SLAs.
  • Cloud and AI infrastructure: reserved capacity plus engineering services; data residency in Canada.
  • mRNA bio-foundry modules and cleanroom fit-out: fixed-price modular contract with commissioning milestones (Xtalks, 2022).
  • Far-UVC fixtures and electrical installation: pilot batch followed by scale contract, contingent on efficacy testing (Far UV Technologies, 2026).
  • At-home molecular test strips: subsidized procurement with validation lot testing at UFV.

Facility planning assumption: secure data, sample-handling, and validation space may be required. Site, containment, safety, and cost requirements have not been established. The current allocations assume these needs can be accommodated; feasibility studies must confirm this before funding approval.

Testing and cost of quality

Proposed component Potential test type Draft target (to validate) Quality activity
Data platform User acceptance testing with Fraser Health; penetration testing; failover drill. Zero critical defects; 99.9% uptime in 30-day burn-in. Appraisal + prevention
Bio-foundry Validation batches against reference standards; contamination controls. ≥3 consecutive passing batches before operational release. Appraisal
Far-UVC Third-party pathogen inactivation testing in occupied-room mock-ups. ≥90% airborne inactivation at design dose. Appraisal
At-home tests Clinical sensitivity/specificity validation at UFV lab. ≥95% sensitivity vs. PCR reference. Appraisal
Surveillance network End-to-end signal-to-alert drills; sensor calibration audits. ≥95% site uptime; 48-hour alert standard met in drills. Prevention + appraisal

These quality criteria are examples for planning, not approved specifications yet. A quality plan will define verification methods, safety and privacy controls, independent reviewers, and corrective-action thresholds after approval. The cost and consequences of failures have not been quantified in this draft as well.
