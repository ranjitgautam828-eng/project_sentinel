Fraser Valley Sentinel · $1 billion pandemic preparedness initiative

06 · Life Cycle, Deliverables & Charter

Hybrid waterfall–iterative model, and what “done” looks like

Why a hybrid model

Schwalbe (2015) notes that predictive (waterfall) and iterative (spiral) life cycles suit different kinds of work, and that no single model fits every project.

The project has two fundamentally different risk profiles. Physical infrastructure, including sensor installation, bio-foundry construction, and Far-UVC retrofits, is sequential, dependency-heavy, and expensive to rework. It suits a waterfall approach with gate reviews. Software and research, including the data platform, AI anomaly detection, and the prototype library, are exploratory and benefit from iterative/spiral cycles with frequent validation. Trying to force both into a single model would either slow the research or destabilize the construction schedule.

The hybrid model therefore runs a waterfall spine for infrastructure and spiral increments for the platform and prototype work, synchronized at quarterly integration points. This is visible in the Gantt chart above: the bio-foundry and sensor installation proceed in sequential phases, while the data platform and prototype library iterate across multiple quarters.

Proposed deliverables

Deliverables and acceptance targets are indicative and depend on feasibility, regulatory review, partner agreements, and funding approval.

  1. Operational Fraser Valley Sentinel Surveillance Network: continuous sequencing at wastewater and travel-node sites, with a 48-hour alert standard.
  2. Pathogen Data Platform: controlled data access, anomaly review, partner interfaces, and public summaries only if approved by governance and privacy review.
  3. Prototype Pathogen Library: ≥10 viral-family candidates at Phase-1-ready status.
  4. One operational modular mRNA bio-foundry: validated for local surge production.
  1. Far-UVC clean-air installations: 50+ high-risk rooms, with third-party efficacy validation.
  2. At-home molecular test subsidy program: validated tests, subsidized access, distribution infrastructure.
  3. Workforce development pipeline: trained bioinformaticians, lab technicians, and project staff.
  4. Final project report and sustainability plan: operational handover to Fraser Health/BCCDC, with the research platform retained at UFV.

Project charter (condensed visual)

Purpose

Develop a regional early-warning and rapid-response capability intended to improve preparedness and reduce response delays. Containment and economic outcomes cannot be guaranteed.

Objectives

Proposed targets for validation: a 48-hour verified alert, a 21-day sequence-to-batch for a matching prototype family, at least 50 clean-air rooms, and at least 10 prototype families. The assignment's fictional go-live date is December 31, 2029. It does not establish a validated handover deadline.

Budget & schedule

The $1,000,000,000 allocation is a course-scenario assumption. The proposed Q1 2027 start requires feasibility review. The fictional go-live date is December 31, 2029, not a validated project-close date.

Champion & success criteria

Potential institutional champion: UFV, subject to agreement. Success measures and the future operator require partner approval.

Pre-initiation tasks

  • Complete a needs assessment and Fraser Valley risk mapping before charter approval.
  • Begin partner-led engagement with Fraser Health, BCCDC, the First Nations Health Authority, and affected First Nations; do not presume agreement.
  • Feasibility study for bio-foundry siting and modular procurement options.
  • Data-sharing and privacy impact assessment for the pathogen platform.
  • Confirmation of funding availability and phased disbursement schedule.
