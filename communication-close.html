Closing process

Closing is not an afterthought in a preparedness project, because the risk does not end when the project does. The proposed closing phase has five components.

1. Deliverable acceptance

Formal sign-off against the charter’s success criteria: network operational, platform live, ≥10 prototype families, bio-foundry validated, 50+ clean-air rooms, subsidy program evaluated.

2. Financial reconciliation

Reconcile actual costs against the approved baseline, including authorized contingency use and variance explanations.

3. Operational handover

Transfer operations only to an organization that has agreed to accept them, with a funded operating plan and documented handover.

4. Lessons learned

Document lessons with participating partners; consider publishing a reviewed, non-sensitive evaluation where all required approvals are in place.

5. Benefits review

If approved and resourced, review intended benefits after handover against a defined baseline; report limitations and lessons transparently.

The course scenario uses December 31, 2029, as a fictional go-live date, not a validated schedule commitment. The project close date and operational handover plan would be established during detailed planning.

Proposal summary: The initiative would combine earlier warning, adaptable countermeasure research, and regional manufacturing to strengthen preparedness. The proposed targets are a 48-hour verified alert and a 21-day validated batch for a matching prototype family. These targets are unvalidated and do not guarantee outbreak containment or prevent economic disruption.
